Assessment

100 points, released in three windows. There is no final exam — the course concludes with the capstone project.

Component Points What it is
Capstone 40 A responsible AI-adoption plan for a company, in groups of five: map how work flows, find where AI helps and where it is an expensive distraction, cost it, govern it, and defend it in a report and a presentation. Staged as M1 process map · 5, M2 opportunities and ROI · 5, M3 governance and ethics · 5, final report and presentation · 25.
Exams 35 Two written exams — Exam 1 · 20 and Exam 2 · 15. Open questions and multiple choice. Closed device, no AI. Both fall before the final block.
Seminars 15 Your group presents three times, 5 points each, leading the room on that day's reading. The schedule above names the group and the turn — Seminar 2 (G4) is G4's second of three.
Participation 10 A paragraph at the end of each session on what you learned that day · 8 points, excluding the day your group presents. Plus peer evaluation inside your group · 2 points, calculated as the average of the marks your groupmates give you.

The three windows

Window Points Assessed
1 30 Exam 1 · M1 · Seminar 1
2 30 Exam 2 · M2 · Seminar 2 · Participation
3 40 Final report and presentation · M3 · Seminar 3 · Participation

The windows are checkpoints on one road, not three separate races. A gap in one window is not recoverable in the next, because each window carries its own points — this is deliberate, so that small gaps do not stay invisible until it is too late to fix them.

Participation is countable, not an impression: every one of its 10 points is an artifact. Contribution is assessed, not volume — being quiet by temperament and writing a good paragraph every week earns full marks.

Ground rules

What is asked of you

  • Be here, on time. Attendance is taken in the first five minutes of every session.
  • Read before you arrive. The session is not where the material is delivered — it is where it is argued about. Arriving unread means spending the session as a spectator.
  • Talk. Ask the obvious question and disagree out loud, with a reason. A wrong answer offered is worth more than a right one withheld.
  • Carry your group. You present three times, and peer evaluation inside the group counts towards your participation mark.
  • Bring the outside world in. News, an internship, a family business — the best sessions are often the ones you redirect.

What you can hold me to

  • Class starts on time and ends on time.
  • Every reading is free — open access or SKEMA Learning. You will buy nothing for this course.
  • Grades and feedback within two weeks of submission.
  • I will say when something is contested rather than settled, and much of this field is contested. If I do not know, I will say so and find out before the next session.
  • No gatekeeping: the technical content is taught to be understood by this audience, not to reward prior exposure.

Using AI in this course

AI tools are allowed and encouraged. You are training to manage in a world where refusing to use them would be professional malpractice. Three conditions apply.

  1. Disclose. Any submitted work that used AI carries a short note: which tool, what for. Two lines at the end is enough — "used Claude to restructure section 3 and to find counter-arguments I'd missed." Disclosure never costs marks. Undisclosed use is an academic integrity matter.
  2. You remain responsible. If the model invents a statistic, a court case or a source, that is your error and not the tool's — you signed it. This is the standard you will be held to as a manager: you do not get to blame the system you deployed.
  3. It must not replace your thinking. Use it to attack your argument, to find the objection you missed, to translate, to tidy prose. Not to have the idea for you.

Exams are AI-free — both of them, closed device.

How a session runs

A group presents the day's reading · we run a case, real or simulated · you write for ten minutes on what you learned · project time with your group.

You leave every session having written something. That paragraph is the participation artifact.
