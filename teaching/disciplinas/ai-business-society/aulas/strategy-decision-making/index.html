Before the hierarchy, a definition
Strategy is a purposive process through which decision makers select actions designed to bring about a desired future, varyingly defined as the achievement of a goal (Chandler 1962), the occupation of a valuable position (Porter 1986, 1990), or the resolution of a problem (Rumelt 2011).
Csaszar, Lee, Zemsky & Zenger

Which is why the hierarchy is a causal one
As humans practice and theorize about it, strategic decision making is, therefore, a causal enterprise, one that presupposes beliefs — often tacit beliefs — about how particular initiatives, investments, or policies generate desired outcomes.

Strategy and Decision Making · 4/26

Level 1 · Analytic–predictive — what will happen if X continues?

  • Object of reasoning: associations and trendsinside a model structure and action space that already exist
  • Example: capacity, inventory or hiring against projected demand"The strategist need not know the precise causal mechanism behind sales growth; simply recognizing forecast growth is sufficient" (p. 4)
  • AI generates forecasts, detects patterns, quantifies uncertainty"AI is particularly powerful at this level" — ML detects regularities, LLMs extract signal from unstructured data (p. 4)
  • The human decides which outcomes matter, and how a forecast becomes action"Decisions about relevance, risk, and action continue — at least for now — to rest with human judgment" (p. 4)
Strategy and Decision Making · 5/26

Level 2 · Intervention-oriented — what happens if we do X?

  • Object of reasoning: interventions on variablesactions familiar to the firm or to close analogs, with observable data
  • Example: product expansion, marketing increases, acquisitions"the effort is no longer forecasting trends but forecasting causal effects" (p. 5)
  • AI estimates treatment effects, supports experiments, compares interventionsexperimentation platforms, causal inference techniques, ML on actions by the firm or close analogs (p. 5)
  • The human sets objectives and candidate actions, tests assumptions, weighs trade-offs"At present, humans still act as intervention judges at Level 2" (p. 5)
Strategy and Decision Making · 6/26

Level 3 · Counterfactual–extrapolative — what would happen if we did Y instead?

  • Object of reasoning: counterfactual outcomes of a modelactions the firm has not taken, carried in from another industry, geography or regulatory regime
  • Example: Apple — chip integration, from smartphones to laptopsalso a shift to a platform model · a new pricing mechanism for the industry · entry under a different institutional regime (p. 5)
  • AI surfaces analogies, synthesises external evidence, explores counterfactualsorganises evidence from external firms, industries, geographies; simulates transporting a causal relationship across contexts (p. 5)
  • The human judges which analogies transfer, and sets the risk bounds"arbiters of AI-generated extrapolations … whether contextual differences constrain their valid transport" (p. 5)
Strategy and Decision Making · 7/26

Level 4 · Model-generative — what model would make Z possible?

  • Object of reasoning: causal models of a novel statebuilds the model, rather than applying or extending one — redefining what actions are conceivable
  • Example: Walton composing Walmart · Jobs designing the Macintosh"architecting entirely new futures, wholly distinct from the observable present" (p. 5)
  • AI recombines concepts, stress tests assumptions, compares alternative modelsLLMs "may highlight critical steps to accomplish or subproblems that must be solved", surface implicit assumptions (pp. 5–6)
  • The human frames the new problem, judges coherence and fit, authorises the commitment"At Level 4, humans act as strategic theorists" (p. 6)
Strategy and Decision Making · 8/26

Where this leaves AI on the ladder

  • Level 1 is where AI is strongest — association is what it does best"AI is particularly powerful at this level" (p. 4); abundant tools already exist for Levels 1–2 (p. 12)
  • At Levels 3–4 it assists — it does not author the modelthe paper does NOT say AI fails here: "At Level 4, AI can assist with model construction" (p. 5)
  • The obstacle at Level 4 is data, not intelligencea new model describes a future "wholly distinct from the observable present" (p. 5) — nothing has generated data about it yet
  • Framing the problem, and governing the commitment, stay human"humans retain authority over problem framing and strategic commitment" (Table 2 notes, p. 4)

Strategy and Decision Making · 19/26

What governs the pace of delegation — four conditions

  • i. Feedback density / verifiability"can we score decisions credibly and repeatedly?"
  • ii. Downside boundedness / reversibility"can errors be contained by guardrails, budgets, or the ability to reverse course?"
  • iii. Objective specifiability"can we state what 'good' means without Goodharting ourselves?"
  • iv. Accountability / liability"can we assign responsibility and audit the system's behavior?"

Tier 1 · Informational support — the AI as analyst

  • Back to Challenge 1. The AI is strictly a sensor
  • It delivers the report: demand up in rain, couriers short
  • It takes no action. A human reads the alert and decideswhether to open a financial incentive so couriers ride in the storm
  • Governance is light — errors are visible and correctable
Strategy and Decision Making · 23/26

Tier 2 · Tactical recommender — the AI as associate

  • Back to Challenge 2. The AI proposes three routes, not oneA: R$ 10 coupon for lapsed users · B: free delivery above R$ 30 · C: …
  • It writes the premortem: why a mass send erodes margin
  • It advises. It does not have the send button
  • The board weighs the trade-offs and authorises
Strategy and Decision Making · 24/26

Tier 3 · Bounded operator — the AI as unit manager

  • The board writes the rule. The AI executes it without asking
  • Up to R$ 5 more on the delivery fee when the queue passes 15 minutes
  • Hard stop: R$ 10,000 of subsidy a day
  • Why this is safe — specific objective · instant feedback · a cap a human set
Strategy and Decision Making · 25/26

Tier 4 · Strategic executive — the AI as CEO

  • Back to Challenges 3 and 4. The AI concludes the model is saturated
  • It proposes, on its own: buy DroneFly for R$ 5m, close 40% of the kitchens
  • Would you let it sign?
  • Feedback takes years. The downside is existential
  • What it can do here is simulate and stress test. The signature stays human
