Write me the rule for "this customer will cancel"

The inversion

06

Three kinds of learning

Which one fits depends on what you already recorded.

How Machine Learning Works · 30/58

Supervised — you already have the right answer from the past

  • Classification — the answer is a categorywill this customer cancel? is this transaction fraud? is this ticket a complaint, a question or praise?
  • Regression — the answer is a numberwhat is this property worth? what is March demand? how many days until delivery?
  • Both demos today were supervised classificationby far the most common family in business — the only one that works when you already recorded the outcome
How Machine Learning Works · 31/58

Unsupervised — nobody knows the right answer

  • The system finds natural groups of customers nobody defined in advance
  • It discovers what tends to travel together in the shopping basket
  • It does not run by itselfit returns one grouping out of many possible ones. A person decides whether it is commercially useful and what to call each group. The machine does not know what "premium customer" means.
How Machine Learning Works · 32/58

Reinforcement — decide, observe, adjust

  • The system acts, sees the consequence, gets a reward or a penalty, adjusts next round
  • Dynamic pricing, routing, inventory, robotics
  • It is not the "advanced level" — it is a different fitit needs a clear scoreboard and a cheap place to fail thousands of times. Hence games and simulation; rarely business, where each mistake costs a real customer.
How Machine Learning Works · 33/58

And what kind of machine? Four families, in broad strokes.

FamilyHow it decidesWhat it costs you
Trees & forests a chain of yes/no questions one tree you can read; three hundred you cannot
Regression a weighted sum — one weight per variable assumes a simple shape; you do the thinking about variables
Neural networks many layers of weights, learned expensive, opaque, data-hungry
Neighbours & clusters looks at who resembles whom no model to inspect; slow as the base grows

Which of the two errors hurts more?

  • Fraud check that blocks a legitimate purchasefurious customer, lost sale — the false alarm is the expensive one
  • Predictive maintenance that misses the failurethe whole line stops — here it is the miss that is expensive
  • CV screening that discards a good candidatean invisible and permanent cost — nobody ever measures this one
  • There is no universal answer. There is only your answer, in writing.and it has a price — which is the next slide
How Machine Learning Works · 41/58

The model never says “cancel”. It says a number.

  • For every customer it returns a probability — 0.03, 0.41, 0.87that is all it does. It never decides anything.
  • Somebody has to draw a line and say “from here up, we act”that line is the threshold — and it is not in the data
  • The library ships with the line at 0.50not because 0.50 is right for you. Because it had to ship with something.

Hold on fiber optic for a second

  • The model found that fiber customers cancel more. It does not know why.is it price? service quality? competitors targeting exactly that customer?
  • Three explanations, three completely different action plans — and the machine picks none of themthe machine finds the correlation; a person proposes the cause and owns the decision
  • Confuse the two and you send discounts to the wrong customer for a year

Now change the target

  • Instead of "will cancel", imagine "was hired"and instead of contract type, imagine zip code, or school name, or years without formal employment
  • The mechanism is identical to the one you just watched workexcept now it is learning who the company hired over ten years — including everything recruiters did without ever writing it down
  • “Just delete the gender column”delete it. Zip code, school, sport and part-time patterns remain. You did not remove the bias — you removed it from your own audit and left it running.
  • The arithmetic is neutral. The history is not.and choosing the target — "was hired" rather than "performed well" — is a design decision, not the data's fault

Where projects die

And the page you take to your internship.

How Machine Learning Works · 53/58

Four causes, one sentence each

  • Badly defined problemstarts with "we want to use AI" instead of a specific decision. Ends in an eternal pilot.
  • Bad or scattered dataduplicate records, fields filled in any old way. No model fixes that.
  • No process ownerthe model flags a risk and nobody knows who calls the customer. Prediction without action is not value.
  • No trust from the teamif the person at the front line doesn't understand the recommendation, they ignore the screen and follow their gut
How Machine Learning Works · 54/58

Accountability

  • Bias enters through the history and through the choice of target — and leaves at scale, looking neutral
  • Training a model on a customer base is processing personal data, with a declared purpose and a limit of use
  • The data subject may request review of a decision taken solely by automated processing
  • “System error” is not an institutional answerthe organisation that adopted the decision answers for it
How Machine Learning Works · 55/58

Six questions — take this page with you

  • Which specific decision will improve?if the answer is generic, the project does not exist yet
  • Do we have history with the outcome recorded?
  • Which error is more expensive for us — false alarm or missed?
  • Who acts when the model flags something?name, role, process
  • How do we measure the gain in money, against the way we do it today?
  • Who reviews it when someone complains?
How Machine Learning Works · 56/58
The model does not decide. It offers a calibrated bet. The decision stays with the organisation — with a name, a criterion and accountability.
